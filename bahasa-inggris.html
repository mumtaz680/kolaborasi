English Collaboration

Observation Results of Waste Recycling

About This Collaboration

In English class, we conducted observation activities on waste and composed observation reports. Each team member provided a unique perspective in observing and analyzing the potential use of used CDs as the basic material for making decorative lamps.

Team Observation Results

Ayu Widyastuti

Analyst

How Used CDs Can Be Transformed Into Decorative Lamps

Electronic waste is one of the environmental problems around us that is difficult to decompose, an example of which is used CDs. Used CDs are often thrown away carelessly when they are scratched or can no longer be used. In fact, used CDs contain plastic material that is difficult to decompose and can pollute the environment. One creative effort to overcome this problem is by utilizing used CDs into a new product that has a use value, namely a decorative lamp. This transformation is a real example of an activity in everyday life, namely recycling waste that is no longer used into useful goods.

The transformation process begins when used CDs that are no longer used are collected and separated from their plastic cases. The CD pieces are then thoroughly cleaned to remove dust, stains, and remaining stickers. At this stage, there is a change in the function of the CD, which previously functioned as a storage medium for data, music, and video, now changes into the main raw material for making crafts. This change in function is the core of the recycling process.

The clean CDs are then combined and arranged to form a new product. Through a process of stacking and gluing with special glue, the pieces of CDs form a sturdy tube or lampshade. The shiny surface of the CD that can reflect light creates a unique and attractive rainbow color effect when exposed to light. After all the pieces are firmly attached, a series of cables and small LED lights are installed in the middle. Thus, the components that were previously separated are now united into one whole decorative lamp product that is ready to use.

The final result of this process is a decorative lamp made from used CDs that has both functional and aesthetic value. This product not only functions as a room lighting tool, but also as a decoration that can beautify the interior of the house. In addition to having economic value, this transformation also has a positive impact on the environment because it can reduce the amount of plastic waste and extend the life of used goods. Therefore, recycling used CDs into decorative lamps is one of the creative and environmentally conscious steps to support the creation of a cleaner and more sustainable environment.

How Used CDs Are Transformed into Decorative Lamps

Waste is one of the environmental problems that continues to increase along with technological development and human needs. One type of waste commonly found is used CDs (Compact Discs) that are no longer used because of the development of digital storage media. Although they are considered waste, used CDs actually still have the potential to be turned into useful products. The shiny surface of CDs, which can reflect light, makes them suitable materials for creating decorative lamps. By using creativity and simple materials, used CDs can be transformed into decorative lamps that have aesthetic, functional, and economic value. This activity is also a form of environmental awareness through the reuse of discarded materials.

The process of making decorative lamps begins by collecting unused CDs. The collected CDs are then cleaned with a cloth to remove dust, dirt, and fingerprints. After being cleaned, the CDs are prepared according to the desired lamp design. Next, supporting materials such as LED lights, cables, lamp holders, glue, and other additional materials are prepared. The used CDs are then cut or arranged according to the design requirements while paying attention to safety during the process. At this stage, CDs that were previously unused begin to change their function into the main materials for creating decorative lamp products.

The cleaned and prepared CDs are then assembled into a decorative lamp according to the planned design. The formation process involves arranging several CD pieces using adhesive to create an attractive and sturdy structure. The CD pieces can be arranged in circular patterns, layered shapes, or other patterns to produce a unique design. After the CD structure is formed, LED lights are installed in the prepared section as the light source. LED lights are chosen because they produce attractive lighting and are energy-efficient. The shiny surfaces of the CDs reflect the light from the lamps, creating a beautiful sparkling effect. Once all the components are properly, securely, and safely assembled, the decorative lamp made from used CDs is ready to be used as an attractive craft product.

The final result of this process is a decorative lamp made from used CDs with a creative, unique, and beautiful design. This product not only functions as an additional light source but can also be used as a decoration to make rooms more attractive, such as bedrooms, study rooms, and living rooms. Besides having aesthetic value, decorative lamps made from used CDs also have economic potential because they can be developed into craft products with selling value. Making this product helps reduce the amount of waste that is simply thrown away and increases people's awareness of the importance of reusing materials that can still be utilized. Therefore, transforming used CDs into decorative lamps is a form of creativity and innovation in turning waste into useful products with economic value while supporting a cleaner and more sustainable environment.

How an Old CD Is Transformed into a Lamp

The fast growth of technology has created a new problem for the environment, which is electronic waste. One example of this waste is the compact disc, or CD. In the past, CDs were widely used to store songs, films, and school data. Today, many people no longer use them because they prefer flash drives and the internet. As a result, a lot of old CDs are left unused in drawers or thrown away. Even though they are no longer used, CDs still have a shiny layer that can be reused for decoration. By changing old CDs into a lamp, we can reduce waste and create a product that is both useful and attractive. This idea proves that something that is considered useless can still have a new life.

The process starts by collecting old CDs that are no longer used. The CDs are separated from their cases and checked one by one. The CDs that are still in good condition are chosen, then washed with clean water and wiped with a soft cloth until they are shiny again. In this step, the CDs no longer work as a storage device. Their role changes into a material for decoration. At the same time, the other materials are also prepared. A PVC pipe is measured and cut to form the stand, then filled with sand and heated so it can be bent into an L shape without breaking. All of these preparations must be done carefully so the final lamp will be strong, safe, and neat.

After all materials are ready, they are put together to form a lamp. Some CDs are stacked and glued to make the base so the lamp can stand firmly. The bent PVC pipe is then attached to the base as the main support. Other CDs are arranged around the bulb to make a lampshade. Because the surface of a CD is reflective, the light from the bulb will spread and shine beautifully when the lamp is switched on. The cable is inserted through the PVC pipe, and the lamp fitting is placed at the end of the pipe. This makes the lamp look tidy and safe to use. When the glue is dry and all parts are attached tightly, the lamp is complete and ready to be displayed.

In the end, old CDs are successfully changed into a lamp that can be used every day. This lamp is not only useful for lighting a room, but also beautiful as a decoration. Its sparkling reflection makes the room look more interesting and different from a normal lamp. This project also has a positive impact on the environment because it reduces the number of CDs that end up as waste. It also trains students to be more creative, careful, and responsible for the environment. In addition, the lamp can be sold as a handmade product, so it can also help to improve economic skills. Therefore, turning old CDs into a lamp is a simple but meaningful way to protect the environment.
